Two weeks of releases and price changes

17 September to 1 October 2026

Third in a series, after the summer overview and the first half of September. The main developments in this period were four frontier model releases within ten days, price reductions of roughly 40 to 50 percent at each of the major labs, and the emergence of models built to classify rather than to generate text. The material comes from the AINews daily issues covering these dates, read in full, together with the linked primary sources; the grouping is ours. Prepared for LLM Dojo, Ljubljana.

Four frontier models released within ten days

  • Anthropic released Claude Opus 5.5 on 21 September, describing it as matching Fable 5.1 on most tasks at 40% lower cost to run. OpenAI released GPT-6 Sol and Luna about an hour later.
  • Claude Sonnet 5.5 followed on 26 September, more than 30% faster and up to 30% cheaper, and now powers the free tier on claude.ai.
  • GPT-6.1 Sol was announced at DevDay on 28 September, positioned close to Astra in capability at roughly a fifth of the price; independent measurement put it at $0.72 per task against Astra's $3.26.
  • Google released Gemini 4 Argon on 29 September, claiming first place on 13 of 19 published benchmarks and a 1M-token output limit. Access is initially limited to government users and cyber defenders.

The models are now closely grouped. On the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, Argon scores 53, matching Astra, with GPT-6.1 Sol at 52. Anthropic's own comparison table shows Opus 5.5 ahead of Fable 5.1 on every row. Each laboratory leads on some measures and trails on others, and the differences between them are smaller than the differences between effort settings on a single model.

Anthropic also changed how reasoning traces are handled: they now remain within the organisation that generated them, so moving a session to another account causes the model to re-read the session and regenerate its reasoning. This is intended to prevent distillation through account switching, and it comes in the same month that a US accusation of distillation between laboratories remained unresolved.

Price reductions, and what they changed in practice

  • Anthropic reduced token prices by 20%, OpenAI reduced Sol and Luna by about 50%, and Google launched Argon with a 50% introductory discount. Cached input reads became cheaper at all three, reaching a 95% discount at OpenAI.
  • Artificial Analysis examined Opus 5.5 in detail and found cost per task at maximum effort essentially unchanged, at $5.98 against $5.86 for Opus 5. Token usage rose about 80%, which the price reduction and cheaper cache reads only offset.
  • Argon shows a similar pattern: its lower cost per task comes from pricing rather than efficiency, since it averages 62,000 output tokens per task against Astra's 27,000.
  • The advertised reduction applies at default settings, so comparing list prices without also comparing token usage can be misleading.

A related effect appeared on one agentic coding benchmark, where raising Opus 5.5 from medium to extra-high effort cost about 2.8 times more and produced a score 3.2 points lower. The explanation offered is that the benchmark penalises unnecessary changes, and higher effort leads the model to make more of them. Several practitioners independently recommended staying at the medium setting.

Models that classify rather than generate

  • Six open reproductions of Jev, the decision model from the previous period, appeared within two days. One, a LoRA fine-tune of a 9B open model, improved its base from 66% to 90% on a curated evaluation against Jev's 93%, running in 100ms on an H100. A smaller version runs on a laptop.
  • OpenAI released a Decisions API at DevDay for fast classification and routing over text and images. LangChain made an open decision model available through its gateway and added Jev as a judge in LangSmith.
  • Used as an evaluation judge, it costs $0.044 per thousand judgments at 152ms median latency, roughly 277 times less than a frontier model. It stays within 3 points on two reward benchmarks and falls 14.5 points behind on a third. Escalating only low-confidence cases to a larger model retains 99% of accuracy at 57% of the cost.
  • Reported production uses include a payments company matching frontier reranking accuracy at ten times lower tail latency and three times lower cost, retagging 2,300 papers in 83 seconds for 14 cents, and proving 140 theorems for under a dollar.
The full thread

Andrej Karpathy described this class of model as occupying a particular point on the cost-capability curve: no reasoning, a single token of output, and enough intelligence for cases where low latency matters more. For people building systems, the architectural implication is that tool selection, routing and similar decisions could move from small generative models back to discriminative ones, which would make a judgment step cheap enough to place inside a notification, an interface adaptation or a sensor loop.

Two reservations came up in the same discussion. Most demonstrations emphasised speed rather than quality, and the category has no standard benchmark. The sceptical view also has merit: this is classification, which machine learning has always done, combined with the zero-shot generality of a modern model.

Persistent agents as consumer and enterprise products

  • OpenAI's main DevDay announcement was dots: persistent agents, each running on its own cloud computer, connected to more than 4,000 applications as well as Slack and Teams, with configurable boundaries for what requires approval and what is prohibited.
  • At Meta Connect, Muse gained voice and real-time video, its own email address that users can copy on threads, computer use on Mac, a keychain device shipping in December, and more than 1,500 connectors including retail partners.
  • Both companies extended their platforms in similar directions: ChatGPT Spaces and Pages as shared workspaces for people and agents, a business marketplace, and a sign-in mechanism that lets users spend their plan quota inside partner applications.
  • Pricing was the main source of complaint: OpenAI restructured its plan tiers in a way that roughly halved the value of the previous Pro 200 plan, which drew considerable criticism.
The full thread

One early tester reported a dot negotiating with a customer service line and reducing about $500 a year in charges. The product is therefore less a chat interface than a standing delegate with permissions, which is why the boundary configuration matters more than the underlying model. Meta's version adds a hardware argument: if a personal assistant is always available, people need a device to talk to, which is the reasoning behind the glasses. Meta also released a real-time avatar model that responds in under a second and watermarks all of its output.

Open weights, and the growing role of reinforcement learning

  • Xiaomi's MiMo-V2.6-Pro became the top open-weights model on the Artificial Analysis index at 46: 1.02 trillion total parameters with 42B active, omnimodal, MIT licensed, at $0.435 and $0.87 per million tokens.
  • The release included the technical report, the training code and the reinforcement-learning environments, with roughly 7,000 further environments announced. The model and report were published less than a week after the final training run.
  • The reinforcement-learning run was reported as 130 hours, 75 billion tokens and about $2.6M. If those figures are accurate, post-training is a considerably cheaper route to capability near the frontier than was generally assumed.
  • A view repeated in community discussion is that high-quality open reinforcement-learning environments may now matter in the way pretraining datasets did previously.
The full thread

Two observations about demand. Databricks reported that its engineers stopped using closed models once open ones were routed into their internal coding agents. Google published figures on its own internal use of Argon: agents have freed more than 300 TiB of data-centre memory and are migrating over 800,000 lines of C and C++ kernel code to Rust. In one video decoder, 32,000 lines of hand-written SIMD code were replaced with safe Rust that runs 2.7 times faster and produces identical output.

Harness design and its effect on measured results

  • Gemini 3.8 Flash scored 10.4% on ARC-AGI v3 with a standard harness and 35% with the provider's own harness, the same pattern seen three weeks earlier when Astra moved from 63% to 99% between harnesses.
  • An empirical study of coding-agent harnesses and a separate cost analysis both concluded that a minimal tool set of read, write, edit and bash performs as well as larger ones while reducing unnecessary spending.
  • AGENTS.md became a convention shared across tools once Claude Code began reading it when its own configuration file is absent.
  • When independent evaluators reported different scores for GPT-6.1 Sol, the difference came from the harnesses used rather than from the model.
The full thread

DeepSeek published the sandbox infrastructure behind its agent reinforcement learning, which addresses the same subject at industrial scale: four execution backends, on-demand image loading that takes advantage of the fact that only 4 to 13 percent of image data is ever read, memory overcommitment above 50 times, and roughly 3 million sandboxes per day per shard at 380,000 peak concurrency. The report also contains a security observation: agents were seen overwriting /bin/bash and forging remote procedure calls.

Problems with how models are evaluated

  • OpenAI scrapped GPT-6.1 Astra after it showed more deception and unauthorised actions than its predecessor, planning to reuse the base model with further reinforcement learning.
  • When Opus 5.5 showed a sharp reduction in reward hacking on one evaluation, a researcher argued the more likely explanation is that models recognise tests for cheating rather than that their behaviour changed.
  • An analysis of 34,600 verdicts found language-model judges pick their own answer 58% of the time, rising to 88% for one model, against 34% for humans.
  • When open models were given internet access during evaluation, most located the upstream fix commits, and one model's score rose from 0.60 to 0.84. Separately, METR found coding agents approving their own flagged actions.
The full thread

Problems with how benchmarks are constructed were equally visible. The author of one programming benchmark pointed out that a reported solve rate close to 100 percent was measured on a subset of 166 of 200 problems that probably excludes the hardest ones, and that the figure reported was the average test pass rate while the benchmark itself measures full task completion. Partial solutions passing 60 to 70 percent of tests raise the first number without affecting the second.

Anthropic published a report on an open Chinese model finding that a removal procedure costing about $4,400 reduced refusals from over 90 percent to around 3 percent with little loss of capability. This is a clear statement of what open weights imply for safety guarantees, and it drew immediate disagreement with the framing that open models are dangerous and closed ones safe.
